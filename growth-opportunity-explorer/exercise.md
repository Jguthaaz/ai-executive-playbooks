# Exercise — choosing a growth hypothesis to investigate
**Status: Work in Progress · Hypothetical · No discovery conducted**

[Project home](README.md) · [Portfolio guide](../PORTFOLIO-GUIDE.md)

## Decision and boundary
Fictional Northstar ServiceCo supplies B2B maintenance services. Assume it can investigate one growth hypothesis over four weeks. The company name, segments and commercial assumptions below are constructed, not market observations.

Objective: identify an important customer problem, a credible offer and an economical path to serving it. AI is optional. This explores customer-facing growth, whereas the existing quotation case explores an internal workflow.

## Segment and opportunity hypotheses
| ID | Segment / buyer role | Job and assumed unmet need | Proposed value proposition | Alternatives to investigate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | Existing single-site customers / operations manager | Coordinate recurring maintenance without repeated administrative work | Scheduled maintenance bundle with clear service scope and reporting | Ad hoc purchases, internal scheduling, competitor contracts |
| G2 | Mid-sized multi-site operators / regional operations leader | Obtain consistent service visibility across sites | Coordinated service plan with consolidated reporting and escalation | Separate local suppliers or an internal coordination team |
| G3 | Prospective customers with aging equipment / maintenance lead | Identify preventable disruption before buying services | Fixed-scope diagnostic assessment with prioritized actions | In-house inspection, equipment vendor assessment, deferral |

None of these buyers has been interviewed. Customer access, willingness to pay, procurement cycles and competition are unknown. Do not treat the table as segmentation validated by research.

## Opportunity canvas — G1
- **Problem hypothesis:** repeated coordination is costly enough that existing customers value a recurring bundle.
- **Offer:** a defined schedule and response protocol; no uptime guarantee or predictive capability.
- **Value mechanism:** lower coordination burden and greater planning visibility.
- **Commercial mechanism:** recurring service revenue, conditional on retention and delivery economics.
- **Capabilities required:** reliable scheduling, service capacity, scope control and account ownership.
- **Technology role:** start with existing scheduling and reporting tools. Add AI only if a specific, evidenced bottleneck emerges.
- **Critical unknown:** whether customers prefer commitment to flexibility.
- **Failure signal:** customers describe the friction but will not prioritize a paid offer, or delivery requirements erase margin.

## Synthetic commercial illustration
For G1 only, assume a discovery pool of 20 existing accounts, with no claim that these accounts exist.
| Input | Invented assumption |
| --- | ---: |
| Accounts in a potential future offer cohort | 20 |
| Adoption hypothesis | 25% |
| Annual revenue per adopted account | USD 12,000 |
| Direct annual delivery cost per adopted account | USD 8,000 |

Illustrative adopted accounts = 20 × 25% = **5**.
Annual revenue = 5 × USD 12,000 = **USD 60,000**.
Contribution before acquisition, overhead, setup and other omitted costs = 5 × (12,000 − 8,000) = **USD 20,000**.

This is scenario arithmetic, not a pipeline, forecast, market size or profit estimate. It assumes full-year adoption without ramp-up, churn or displacement of existing revenue. At 10% adoption the same calculation gives 2 accounts and USD 8,000 contribution; at 40%, 8 accounts and USD 32,000. If the bundle replaces existing purchases, only incremental revenue and incremental cost belong in the real business case.

## Discovery priority comparison
Use favorable 1–5 ordinal scores with equal weights. These are invented discussion judgments.
Anchors: 1 = weak / difficult / poor; 3 = plausible but uncertain; 5 = strong / straightforward / clear. For evidence access, assess ability to investigate, not evidence already obtained.
| Opportunity | Strategic fit | Customer evidence access | Delivery feasibility | Total / 15 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| G1 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 13 |
| G2 | 5 | 2 | 2 | 9 |
| G3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 10 |

G1 is the provisional discovery candidate because existing-customer access and delivery fit are assumed easier. The matrix does not rank market attractiveness or prove demand. If G1 access falls from 5 to 2, it ties G3 at 10; choosing whom to interview is itself evidence-sensitive.

## Proposed four-week discovery plan
All activities are proposals; no contact, interview or sale has occurred.
| Period | Activity | Evidence / decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Confirm permissions, segment and current offering; prepare non-leading interview questions | Separate renewal, expansion and replacement revenue; establish eligibility |
| Week 2 | Seek authorization to interview six relevant customer roles | Capture recent behavior, workarounds and buying constraints; retain contradictory responses |
| Week 3 | Present a scoped concept and delivery assumptions to willing reviewers | Record tradeoffs, budget ownership and objections; interest is not willingness to pay |
| Week 4 | Evaluate a limited commercial test with operations and finance | Continue, redesign or stop; a paid test requires separate authorization and delivery readiness |

Interview prompts:
- Tell me about the last maintenance coordination problem and its consequence.
- How did you resolve it, and what did it require?
- What is already covered by current contracts?
- Who owns the budget and what would prevent changing the arrangement?
- What would make a recurring bundle less useful than your current approach?

Avoid asking only whether the idea sounds attractive.

## Proposed evidence gates
Illustrative thresholds must be agreed before research, not tuned afterward.
- Continue concept discovery if at least four of six relevant interviewees describe a recent, specific problem with consequences and an existing workaround.
- Consider a small paid test only after budget ownership, scope, delivery capacity and acceptable unit economics are evidenced. Interview counts alone cannot justify investment.
- Redesign if needs differ materially by segment.
- Stop if the bundle merely repackages current purchases without incremental customer value or sustainable contribution.

A small convenience sample cannot establish market prevalence. No statistical generalization is intended.
