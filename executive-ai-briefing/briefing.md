## Recommendation
Investigate process standardization first and test the incremental case for AI. Do not approve AI scale on the current synthetic assumptions. If the sponsor authorizes discovery, cap it at ten working days and two person-days; these are proposed boundaries for this memo, not the separate discovery budget in the investment lab.

## Why this matters
The fictional quotation workflow may contain fragmented content, repeated drafting and approval delays. These are hypotheses. Faster drafting alone does not establish faster customer response, higher conversion or lower spending.

The business question is whether AI adds enough value beyond a curated catalog and standard template to justify its cost and operating burden.

## Options
| Option | Potential advantage | Tradeoff / unresolved question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standardize intake, catalog and templates | Addresses foundations and provides a comparator | May not address approval waiting or exceptional requests |
| Add AI-assisted drafting with human approval | May reduce preparation effort | Source quality, incorrect scope, review burden and recurring costs |
| Defer change while gathering baseline evidence | Avoids premature commitment | Delays learning; does not resolve existing friction if it is material |

All advantages are potential, not observed.

## What the illustrative economics say
The [Value Realization Playbook](../value-realization-playbook/exercise.md) assumes 200 quotations a month, 60% adoption and 15 minutes of incremental improvement versus templates. This implies 30 hours/month of released capacity.

At an assumed USD 40/hour, the capacity equivalent is USD 1,200/month, equal to assumed incremental AI operating cost. It provides no recurring surplus to recover the assumed USD 18,000 setup investment. This is neither cash savings nor a forecast.

Higher adoption or lower review time changes the arithmetic, but those inputs remain untested. Revenue upside is excluded because no causal evidence exists.

## Conditions and decision rights
The sponsor owns resource approval. The process owner owns workflow outcomes; risk and technology review access and controls; finance validates cost and benefit classification.

Proceed to a pilot decision only with an owner, permitted data, controlled critical errors, an evaluation plan against the template and a credible full-cost estimate. Specify numerical quality and review thresholds before testing.

Pause if no source owner exists, material errors cannot be contained, or discovery indicates that process-only improvement should take priority.

## Risks and uncertainty
The main risks are mistaking capacity for savings, ignoring approval delays, understating review/support effort and treating AI as the default solution. No vendor has been selected, system assessed, funding approved or return established.

## What would change the recommendation?
Evidence of materially better total time and quality versus the template, a valuable use for capacity, sustainable operating ownership and positive incremental economics would support reconsideration. Evidence of greater review burden or unmanageable errors would support redesign or stopping.

**Requested committee disposition:** approve bounded discovery / request revisions / defer. Record rationale, dissent, accountable owner and next review date. No disposition has actually been made.

## Reusable executive briefing structure
1. **Decision:** what must be decided, by whom and within what boundary?
2. **Recommendation:** what action follows from the current evidence?
3. **Business relevance:** whose problem matters and why?
4. **Alternatives:** include a simpler option and deferral.
5. **Evidence and economics:** separate observations, assumptions and calculations.
6. **Risks and controls:** what could fail, and what blocks approval?
7. **Next step:** owner, resource boundary, evidence and review trigger.
8. **Change of mind:** what would reverse the recommendation?

Keep the main memo concise. Link detailed models rather than presenting unsupported precision. This fictional memo and its structure were prepared with AI assistance.
